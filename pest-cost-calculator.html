Your rabbit control options

  • 1080 baiting

    Reduces rabbit numbers across a property. Baits are laid with a bait layer.

  • Pindone baiting

    An alternative where 1080 is not suitable.

  • Calicivirus release

    A biological control, released ahead of baiting. Best released from late summer to early autumn, before the break of season.

  • Warren fumigation

    Treats active warrens, and is used as follow-up where burrows reopen after other control.

  • Warren ripping

    Destroying warrens is the most important part of lasting rabbit control. Ripping is done when rabbit numbers are at their lowest, usually in summer and after baiting. It needs machinery access and care around native vegetation. We can do it, or you can, which we recommend wherever you are able to.

  • Rodenator

    A warren treatment that uses a concussive blast to kill rabbits in the warren. Used where warrens cannot be reached for ripping, and when baiting or virus is not being used.

  • Shooting

    Ground shooting, including thermal night work.

Most properties need a combination. Numbers are brought down first, then the warrens are dealt with so rabbits cannot rebuild. We will be in touch about which options suit your property.

Your fox control options

  • Fox den detection

    A trained detection dog locates dens by scent, so control goes where foxes are breeding.

  • Den fumigation

    Removes a litter at the den in late winter and spring.

  • 1080 baiting

    Controls foxes across large properties and whole districts.

  • Canid pest ejectors

    A target-specific device that stays in place for ongoing protection.

  • Trapping

    For problem foxes, and where baits cannot be used.

  • Shooting

    Ground shooting with thermal optics, often used ahead of lambing.

Most properties need a combination, and fox control works best when neighbours act together. We will be in touch about which options suit your property.

Keeping wild birds away from your flock

  • Why it matters

    Wild birds, especially water birds, can spread avian influenza to poultry, by contact or by contaminating where your birds live, feed and drink.

  • Enclosures and netting

    Sheds, secure enclosures or netting keep poultry away from wild birds.

  • Feed and water

    Feed and water are kept under cover where wild birds cannot reach them, and feed spills are cleaned up straight away.

  • Deterrents

    Netting, mesh and deterrents stop wild birds roosting near your poultry.

  • Sick or dead birds

    Call the Emergency Animal Disease Hotline on 1800 675 888.

We assess the site, then put in place the deterrents that keep wild birds off it. We will be in touch about what suits your property. Bird management
