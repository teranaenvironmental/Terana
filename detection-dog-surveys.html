Services / Fox den detection

Fox den detection

Fox dens are hard to find by eye. A dog trained to fox odour locates them by scent, so control effort can be directed to where the dens are.

Send an enquiryCall 0450 303 434

How a survey runs

The survey is handler-led. The dog works off lead ahead of and across the handler's line of travel, covering ground on both sides of the route, and indicates at the source.

Every indication is inspected by the handler, and each den, warren or sign is recorded on the spot with GPS coordinates, a timestamp and photographs.

Handler and dog tracks are logged independently by GPS, giving a record of the ground each covered.

What you receive

  • Survey report

    Effort, area, weather and search conditions, findings and recommendations.

  • Den register

    Every den and warren site with its location, entrances, likely species, activity status and the basis for each call. Built for ongoing monitoring and control.

  • Mapping file

    Survey tracks, boundaries and recorded sites, ready to open in mapping software.

Timing and what happens next

The fox breeding season runs from September to December, when new natal dens are established.

Den surveys, and treating the dens they find, are a crucial part of an integrated program. Interrupting the breeding cycle removes a large number of foxes at one time. It is also the most ethical management method over this period, as other methods risk leaving dependent cubs in a den with no mother to care for them.

Recorded den sites feed directly into control: monitoring, treatment of confirmed dens and follow-up survey.
